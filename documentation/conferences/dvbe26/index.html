ACT II

HITL at the PR: from review to decision

Human review only counts when reviewers see the evidence, own the risk, and can say no.

Why green PRs can still fool humans

Automation bias
Green and polished feels safe
Correlated evidence
The agent writes code and tests
Context gap
Consequences live elsewhere
Queue pressure
Generation outpaces review

A human presence does not guarantee a human judgment.

Meaningful HITL requires sight, accountability, and power to act

See enough
Context + evidence
Be accountable
Competence + authority
Be able to act
Time + intervention

Without them, approval is human-as-a-button.

1 — Classify the change before choosing the review

Start with impact and uncertainty. Autonomy, reversibility, data, rights, safety, sector, and applicable law can raise the level.

REVIEW LEVEL 1
Local and reversible
Docs · formatting · contained tests
Policy-defined review
REVIEW LEVEL 2
Application behavior
Code owner + test evidence
REVIEW LEVEL 3
Cross-system or sensitive
Contracts · schema · personal data · security
Specialist reviewers
REVIEW LEVEL 4
Rights, safety, or regulated operations
Accountable owner + controlled release

The goal is effective control where consequences matter, not approval everywhere.

2 — Expose the rename as an evidence package

Required owners

Classification comes before compliance claims

Regulated AI system

Article 14 requires effective human oversight for applicable high-risk AI systems.

AI-assisted development

Using an AI coding agent does not by itself classify the software being changed as a high-risk AI system.

Classify the deployed system and use case first. Apply human review, intervention, auditability, and rollback as engineering controls regardless.

EU AI Act: classify the system before choosing controls

Route when

An AI system or GPAI model is placed on the EU market, used in the EU, or its output affects people in the EU.

Classify

Provider or deployer · intended purpose · prohibited practice · Annex III/I high-risk signal · transparency · GPAI.

Engineer evidence

Human oversight · model and data lineage · logs · accuracy and robustness tests · monitoring · rollback or disablement.

High-risk milestones: Annex III · 2 Dec 2027; Annex I product AI · 2 Aug 2028. Classification and legal interpretation require qualified owners. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

EU AI Act Article 14 · AI changes financial logic

Risk: an agent-generated change reaches production


// Agent-generated from: "add a 2% refund fee"
BigDecimal refundAmount(Order order) {
    return order.total()
        .multiply(new BigDecimal("0.98"))
        .setScale(2, RoundingMode.DOWN);
}

A green pipeline does not prove the pricing policy, currency, rounding, ledger impact, or customer outcome.

Fix: force HITL to review the change

PR + evidence
↓
Merge blocked
Human review required
↓
Human decides
Approve · modify · reject · stop

Article 14 requires effective human oversight for applicable high-risk AI systems. Apply the same HITL gate to agent-generated financial changes as an engineering control; classify legal applicability separately.

EU AI Act · More Java issues

Examples:

Issue Risk Fix AI Act hook
1 · No human oversight Agent-generated or model-driven change to financial logic reaches production on a green pipeline Merge blocked until a competent reviewer approves, modifies, rejects, or stops; prompt, model version, diff, and tests exposed Art. 14: human oversight
2 · No traceability of AI outputs Model output drives a decision, but model version, input, and result are not recorded Decision log with model version, input reference, output, confidence, and reviewer, retained under a defined policy Art. 12 · 26: logging, deployer log retention
3 · AI interaction not disclosed A chatbot or generated content is presented to users as human or unmarked Disclosure flag in the API and UI; generated content labeled and machine-readable where required Art. 50: transparency obligations
4 · AI feature shipped unclassified A scoring or ranking feature reaches users with no intended-purpose or risk classification Classification record (role, purpose, Annex III signal) required by a CI gate before release Art. 5 · 6: prohibited practices, high-risk classification

GDPR: personal data changes the design boundary

Route when

A Java service collects, stores, transforms, exposes, logs, exports, or deletes data linked to a natural person.

Decide with owners

Purpose and lawful basis · controller or processor role · sensitive data · DPIA · transfers · retention.

Engineer evidence

Data inventory · narrow DTOs · field authorization · rights workflows · deletion propagation · privacy-safe observability.

The GDPR has applied since 25 May 2018.

Privacy, DPO, legal, security, and data owners decide lawful basis, roles, DPIA, transfers, and risk acceptance. Regulation (EU) 2016/679

GDPR · More Java issues (1/2)

Examples:

Issue Risk Fix GDPR hook
1 · Entity exposed by the API findById returns the whole CustomerProfile; every stored field becomes API data Purpose-specific CustomerSummary DTO with authorization and access audit Art. 5(1)(c) · 25: minimisation, data protection by design
2 · PII in logs and traces log.info("Customer {}", customer) writes email and address to logs, traces, and metrics Log ids only; masking layer and field-level redaction policy; no PII in observability Art. 5(1)(f) · 32: confidentiality, security of processing
3 · Erasure not propagated repository.delete(id) removes the row, but copies stay in caches, search indexes, topics, backups, and analytics Erasure workflow that fans out to every store and records completion Art. 17: right to erasure

GDPR · More Java issues (2/2)

Examples:

Issue Risk Fix GDPR hook
4 · Retention never enforced Data kept forever; no retention period or purge job retentionUntil per purpose and a scheduled purge with a recorded outcome Art. 5(1)(e): storage limitation
5 · No access or export path Data subject requests are answered with ad hoc SQL DataSubjectExport gathers data across services into a portable format with an audit trail Art. 15 · 20: access, portability

Cyber Resilience Act (CRA): secure the product lifecycle

Route when

Software or a connected Java component may be a product with digital elements made available on the EU market.

Decide with owners

Product classification · manufacturer role · important or critical category · conformity route · support period.

Engineer evidence

Threat model · secure defaults · SBOM · vulnerability handling · signed updates · user guidance · end-of-support.

Article 14 reporting applies from 11 Sep 2026; full application starts 11 Dec 2027.

Product, product-security, legal, compliance, support, and market-access owners decide applicability and conformity. Regulation (EU) 2024/2847

CRA · More Java issues

Examples:

Issue Risk Fix CRA hook
1 · Secrets in the artifact Hardcoded API key or signing key inside the JAR or properties Inject secrets at runtime; block secrets in the artifact with a build scan Annex I: confidentiality, protection of data
2 · Insecure defaults Default password, open admin surface, or trustAllCerts shipped in the product Secure-by-default config; SecurityDefaults .validate(config) fails on weak settings Annex I: secure by default, minimal attack surface
3 · SBOM drift Fat JAR built with latest or version ranges; no inventory of what shipped Pinned dependency lock; CycloneDX SBOM generated in CI and attached to the release Annex I: SBOM, component transparency
4 · Unsafe deserialization new ObjectInputStream(in) .readObject() on update or plugin metadata Typed DTO with schema validation and an ObjectInputFilter Annex I: limit attack vectors, input validation

NIS2: cybersecurity is a service and supply-chain concern

Route when

A system supports a possible essential or important entity, critical sector, managed service, or digital infrastructure provider.

Decide with owners

Entity and sector classification · Member State law · significant-incident reporting · provider responsibility.

Engineer evidence

Asset inventory · secure configuration · incident workflow · supply-chain review · access control · restore and continuity tests.

EU transposition deadline: 17 Oct 2024; verify the applicable national law.

Legal, security, risk, resilience, continuity, and executive owners decide entity scope and reporting duties. Directive (EU) 2022/2555

NIS2 · More Java issues (1/2)

Examples:

Issue Risk Fix NIS2 hook
1 · Incident as log line logger.error("Security issue", e); no triage, owner, or reporting handoff SecurityIncident classified by severity, evidence captured, owners notified, containment opened Art. 23: significant-incident reporting
2 · Reporting clock ignored Detection time and report deadlines are not recorded ReportingDeadlines.from (incident.detectedAt()) computes the 24h, 72h, and 1-month milestones and alerts owners Art. 23: early warning 24h · notification 72h · final report 1 month
3 · Unvetted supplier dependency Third-party SDK or SaaS client added with no inventory, owner, or review SupplierRegister entry (component, provider, criticality, review, exit plan) enforced by a CI check Art. 21(2)(d): supply-chain security

NIS2 · More Java issues (2/2)

Examples:

Issue Risk Fix NIS2 hook
4 · Overprivileged access Service account or admin API with a static token, no MFA, no least privilege Role-scoped access, short-lived credentials, MFA on admin paths, access logged Art. 21(2)(i, j): access control, MFA
5 · Untested recovery Backup job exists but nobody has restored from it; no continuity runbook Scheduled restore test records RTO and RPO and stores the result as evidence Art. 21(2)(c): continuity, backup, crisis management
6 · Unmanaged vulnerabilities and config Unpatched dependencies and drifting configuration; no asset inventory Asset inventory, CI scanning, patch SLA by severity, and drift detection Art. 21(2)(e, f): vulnerability handling, effectiveness assessment

DORA: prove digital operational resilience

Route when

A Java system supports an in-scope financial entity, important business service, critical ICT function, or ICT provider relationship.

Decide with owners

Applicability · function criticality · incident reporting · provider criticality · outsourcing and concentration risk.

Engineer evidence

ICT inventory · detection and escalation · restore and failover tests · change control · provider monitoring · exit plans.

DORA has applied since 17 Jan 2025.

Legal, compliance, risk, resilience, security, procurement, and business owners decide applicability and risk acceptance. Regulation (EU) 2022/2554

DORA · More Java issues (1/2)

Examples:

Issue Risk Fix DORA hook
1 · No resilience on a critical dependency paymentNetwork.settle(batch) with no timeout, idempotency, bounded retry, circuit breaker, or fallback ResiliencePolicy with timeout, bounded retry, and circuit breaker; idempotent replay and deferAndEscalate Art. 9 · 11: ICT risk management, response and recovery
2 · Incidents not classified or reportable Failure logged as error; no major-incident criteria, clock, or report handoff IncidentClassifier applies the criteria (clients affected, duration, data loss, criticality) and routes to the reporting workflow Art. 17–19: incident classification and reporting
3 · Concentration on one ICT provider One cloud or payment provider hardcoded across the code; no abstraction or exit path Port/adapter boundary plus a ProviderRegister with criticality, alternative provider, and tested exit plan Art. 28 · 30: third-party risk, exit strategies

DORA · More Java issues (2/2)

Examples:

Issue Risk Fix DORA hook
4 · Recovery never tested Failover and restore exist on paper but were never exercised; RTO and RPO unknown Scheduled failover and restore drills record RTO and RPO and store the results as evidence Art. 11 · 24–25: continuity, resilience testing
5 · Uncontrolled ICT change Direct production deploy or config change with no approval, rollback, or audit trail Gated change pipeline with approval, automated rollback, and a change record linked to the release Art. 9: ICT change management
6 · No ICT asset or dependency inventory Unknown libraries, services, and data flows behind an important function Inventory mapping each function to components, dependencies, owner, and criticality, built from the SBOM and service catalog Art. 8: identification, ICT asset mapping

Takeaways

What can a team do next Monday?

  • Label agent-created PRs and record generated artifacts.
  • Add risk triggers to the PR template. (GDPR, CRA, NIS2 & DORA)
  • Route high-impact changes to accountable specialist owners.
  • Require explicit limits, rollback, and monitoring evidence.

What can a team do next Monday?

  • Review incidents and near misses as control failures—not only code failures.
  • Review whether your service databases have migration scripts.
  • Review if team know and use Parallel Change pattern
  • Review whether service communications use governed contracts (OpenAPI, Avro, etc.).

What can a team do next Monday?

  • Review whether your teams are using monorepos to facilitate the AI-agent harness.
  • Review whether your software engineers use common skills.
  • Review whether your squads use SDD or need specific training.
